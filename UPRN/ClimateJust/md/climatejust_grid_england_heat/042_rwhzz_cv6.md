### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - High estimate (90th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which identifies geographic "hot spots" where a community's underlying social vulnerability intersects with projected physical heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing.

#### Description

The metric is an equally-weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** to allow for comparison across England:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid cell. It is calculated by taking the vulnerability scores of neighborhoods (MSOAs), which account for factors like age, health, and housing, and allocating them to grid cells using their population-weighted centre-points.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August). The "2050s" time horizon represents a 20-year average for the period from **2040–2060**.
* **High Estimate (90th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "high" or cautious estimate scenario. Statistically, it indicates there is a **90% probability** that the actual mean summer maximum temperature will be **below** this threshold (and a 10% probability it will be higher).
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **z-score**, indicating how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
