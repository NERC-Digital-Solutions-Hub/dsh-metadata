### Average vulnerability and change in the temperature of the warmest day baseline to 2050s - High estimate (90th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, representing the result of an equally-weighted combination of community characteristics (socio-spatial vulnerability) and the projected physical increase in extreme heat exposure. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where high average social vulnerability intersects with a significant projected rise in temperature for the single hottest summer day, indicating where negative impacts on health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of several key elements, standardized to allow for national comparison:

* **Average Vulnerability:** This component is the simple mean of **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** scores for all neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within a particular **25km grid cell** across England. It reflects the personal, social, and environmental factors, such as age, health, housing, and access to services, that determine a community's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from heat events.
* **Change in the Temperature of the Warmest Day:** Instead of absolute temperatures, this hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **increase** in temperature for the single warmest day of the summer from the 1961–1990 climate baseline to the **2050s** time horizon.
* **High Estimate (90th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "high estimate" scenario where there is a **90% probability** that actual temperature changes will be **below** this threshold (and a 10% probability they will be higher).
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)**. This indicates how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**, with higher positive values representing higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
