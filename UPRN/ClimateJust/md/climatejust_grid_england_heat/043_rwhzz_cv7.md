### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Low estimate (10th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which represents the equally-weighted combination of a community's underlying social vulnerability and its projected physical exposure to high temperatures. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where high social vulnerability intersects with high projected temperatures, indicating areas where negative impacts on community health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of two primary components, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** to allow for comparison across England:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a **25km grid cell**. It is calculated by taking the vulnerability scores of neighborhoods (MSOAs), which account for sensitivity (age and health), enhanced exposure (housing and environment), and the ability to prepare, respond, and recover, and allocating them to the grid cell using their **population-weighted centre-points**.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August). The **2050s** time horizon represents a 20-year average for the period from **2040–2060**.
* **Low Estimate (10th Percentile):** Based on probabilistic climate projections, this represents a conservative or "low" estimate scenario. Statistically, it indicates there is a **90% probability** that the actual mean summer maximum temperature will be **higher** than this value.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how many standard deviations a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. A score of 0 represents the national mean; **higher positive values** indicate higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
