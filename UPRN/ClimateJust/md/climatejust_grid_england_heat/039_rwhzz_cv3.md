### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - High estimate (90th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which identifies geographic "hot spots" where a community's social vulnerability intersects with projected physical heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighbourhood coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing that could occur as a result.

#### Description

The metric is an equally weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** to allow for comparison across England:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid. It is calculated using the population-weighted centre-points of neighbourhoods (MSOAs) to allocate social vulnerability scores, based on factors like sensitivity, housing, and adaptive capacity, to the corresponding grid cell.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August) for the **2050s** time horizon. This period represents the 20-year average for the window from 2040–2060.
* **High Estimate (90th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "high estimate" scenario. Statistically, it indicates there is a **90% probability** that the actual mean summer maximum temperature will be **below** this value.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values indicate higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
