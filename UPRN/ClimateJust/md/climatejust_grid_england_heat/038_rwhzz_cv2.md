### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Central estimate (50th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, identifying geographic "hot spots" where a community's social vulnerability intersects with projected physical heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing.

#### Description

The metric is an equally weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** to allow for national comparison:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid. It is calculated using the population-weighted centre-points of neighborhoods (MSOAs) to allocate social vulnerability scores, based on factors like age, health, and housing, to the corresponding grid cell.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August) for the **2050s** time horizon. This period represents the average for the 20-year window from 2040–2060.
* **Central Estimate (50th Percentile):** Based on probabilistic projections, this represents the **median scenario**. Statistically, it indicates there is an equal probability that actual temperature changes will be higher or lower than this threshold.
* **RECOMMENDED:** This combination of the medium emissions scenario and the central estimate is explicitly identified as the **recommended starting point** for assessing future heat risks.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
