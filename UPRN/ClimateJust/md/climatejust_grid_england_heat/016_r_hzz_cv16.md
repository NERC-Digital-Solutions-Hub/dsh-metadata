### Average vulnerability and change in mean summer maximum temperature baseline to 2050s - Low estimate (10th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, representing the result of an equally-weighted combination of community characteristics and the projected physical increase in heat exposure. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where a neighborhood's average social vulnerability intersects with a projected rise in temperature, indicating where negative impacts on community health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of two primary elements, standardized as a z-score to allow for comparison across England:

* **Average Vulnerability:** This component is the simple mean of **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** scores for all neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within a specific **25km grid cell** across England. It accounts for the personal, social, and environmental factors—such as age, health, housing, and access to services—that determine a community's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from heat events.
* **Change in Mean Summer Maximum Temperature:** Instead of absolute temperatures, this hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **increase** in average daily maximum temperatures during the summer months (June, July, and August) from the 1961–1990 climate baseline to the **2050s** time horizon.
* **Low Estimate (10th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a conservative or "low" scenario where there is a **90% probability** that the actual increase in temperature will be **higher** than this threshold.
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)**, which indicates how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
