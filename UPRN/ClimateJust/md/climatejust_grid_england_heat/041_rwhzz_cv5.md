### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Central estimate (50th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which identifies geographic "hot spots" where a community's social vulnerability intersects with projected physical heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood coming into contact with high temperatures and the severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing that could occur as a result.

#### Description

The metric is an equally-weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** relative to the English average:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents socio-spatial heat vulnerability aggregated to a 25km grid using the population-weighted centre-points of neighborhoods (MSOAs) to allocate the data. It accounts for the personal, social, and environmental factors, such as age, health, and housing characteristics, that determine a community's underlying susceptibility to heat.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August). The "2050s" time horizon represents a 20-year average for the period from **2040–2060**.
* **Central Estimate (50th Percentile):** Based on probabilistic projections, this represents the **median scenario**, indicating there is an equal probability that actual temperature values will be higher or lower than this threshold.
* **RECOMMENDED:** This combination of the medium emissions scenario and the central estimate is explicitly identified by Climate Just and UKCP guidance as the **recommended starting point** for assessing future heat risks.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how many standard deviations a grid cell deviates from the **English average**; higher positive values indicate higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
