### Population weighted vulnerability and change in mean summer maximum temperature baseline to 2050s - Central estimate (50th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which identifies geographic "hot spots" where a community's underlying social vulnerability intersects with the projected physical change in heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood experiencing increased temperatures and the severity of potential negative impacts on community health and wellbeing.

#### Description

The metric represents an equally-weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature change, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** relative to the English average:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid cell. It is calculated by taking the social vulnerability scores of neighborhoods (MSOAs), which account for sensitivity, housing, and adaptive capacity, and allocating them to grid cells using their **population-weighted centre-points**. This ensures the score accurately reflects where the population is located within each 25km area.
* **Change in Mean Summer Maximum Temperature baseline to 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **increase** in average daily maximum temperature during the summer months (June, July, and August). The "change" is measured from the 1961–1990 climate baseline to the **2050s** time horizon (the period from 2040–2060).
* **Central Estimate (50th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents the **median scenario**. Statistically, it indicates there is an equal probability that actual temperature changes will be higher or lower than this value.
* **RECOMMENDED:** This combination of the medium emissions scenario and the central estimate is explicitly identified as the **recommended starting point** for assessing future heat risks.
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **z-score**, indicating how many standard deviations a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
