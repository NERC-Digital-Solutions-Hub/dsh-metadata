### Average vulnerability and change in the temperature of the warmest night baseline to 2050s - Central estimate (50th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, representing the result of an equally-weighted combination of community characteristics (socio-spatial vulnerability) and the projected physical increase in extreme night-time heat exposure. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where high social vulnerability coincides with a significant projected rise in temperature for the single hottest summer night, indicating where negative impacts on community health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of several key elements, standardized as a z-score to allow for national comparison:

* **Average Vulnerability:** This component is the simple mean of **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** scores for all neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within a particular **25km grid cell** across England. It reflects personal, social, and environmental factors, such as age, health, housing, and access to services, that determine a community's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from heat events.
* **Change in the Temperature of the Warmest Night:** Instead of absolute temperatures, this hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **increase** in temperature for the single warmest night of the summer from the 1961–1990 climate baseline to the **2050s** time horizon.
* **Central Estimate (50th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents the **median scenario**, where there is an equal probability that actual temperature changes will be higher or lower than this value.
* **RECOMMENDED:** This combination of a **medium emissions scenario** and the central estimate is explicitly identified as a **recommended starting point** for assessing future heat risks.
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)**. This indicates how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**, with higher positive values representing higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
