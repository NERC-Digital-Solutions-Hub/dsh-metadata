### Average vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Low estimate (10th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, representing the result of an equally-weighted combination of community characteristics and projected physical heat exposure. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where high average social vulnerability intersects with high projected temperatures, indicating where negative impacts on health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of two primary elements, standardized as a z-score to allow for national comparison:

* **Average Vulnerability:** This component is the simple mean of **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** scores for all neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within a specific **25km grid cell** across England. it accounts for the personal, social, and environmental factors, such as age, health, and housing, that determine a community's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from heat events.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature (2050s):** This hazard-exposure metric utilizes projections for the average daily maximum temperatures during the summer months (June, July, and August) for the **2050s** time horizon.
* **Low Emissions Scenario:** Projections are based on a "low emissions" future (RCP2.6) where the world achieves rapid and sustained reductions in greenhouse gas emissions. 
* **Low Estimate (10th Percentile):** Based on probabilistic climate projections (UKCP09), this represents a conservative scenario where there is a **90% probability** that actual temperatures will be higher than this threshold.
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)**. This indicates how far a grid cell deviates from the English average, with higher positive values representing higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
