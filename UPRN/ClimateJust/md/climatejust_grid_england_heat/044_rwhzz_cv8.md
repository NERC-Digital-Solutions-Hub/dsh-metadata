### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Central estimate (50th percentile) RECOMMENDED

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which represents an equally weighted combination of a community's underlying social vulnerability and its projected physical exposure to high temperatures. It identifies geographic "hot spots" by accounting for both the likelihood of neighborhoods coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing.

#### Description

The metric is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** relative to the English average and is composed of the following elements:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a **25km grid cell**. It is calculated by taking the social vulnerability scores of neighborhoods (MSOAs), which account for sensitivity (age and health), enhanced exposure (housing and environment), and the ability to prepare, respond, and recover, and allocating them to the grid cell using their **population-weighted centre-points**.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum summer temperature** (across June, July, and August). The **2050s** time horizon represents a 20-year average for the period from **2040–2060**.
* **Central Estimate (50th Percentile):** Based on probabilistic climate projections, this represents the **median scenario**, indicating there is an equal probability that actual temperature values will be higher or lower than this threshold.
* **RECOMMENDED:** This specific combination of the **medium emissions scenario** and the central estimate is explicitly identified by Climate Just and UKCP guidance as the **recommended starting point** for assessing future heat risks.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how far a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
