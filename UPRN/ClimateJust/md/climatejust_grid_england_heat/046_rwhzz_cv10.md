### Population weighted vulnerability and change in mean summer maximum temperature baseline to 2050s - Low estimate (10th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, identifying geographic "hot spots" where a community’s underlying social vulnerability intersects with the projected physical change in heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood experiencing increased temperatures and the severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing that could occur as a result.

#### Description

The metric represents an equally-weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature change, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** relative to the English average:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid cell. It is calculated using the population-weighted centre-points of neighborhoods (MSOAs) to allocate social vulnerability scores, based on factors like age, health, and housing, to the corresponding grid cell. This method ensures that the vulnerability score for a grid cell more accurately reflects where the population actually lives within that area.
* **Change in Mean Summer Maximum Temperature baseline to 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **increase** in average daily maximum temperature during the summer months (June, July, and August). The "change" is measured from the 1961–1990 climate baseline to the **2050s** time horizon (the period from 2040–2060).
* **Low Estimate (10th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "low" or conservative estimate scenario. Statistically, it indicates there is a **90% probability** that the actual increase in mean summer maximum temperature will be **higher** than this threshold.
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **z-score**. This indicates how many standard deviations a grid cell deviates from the **English average**; higher positive values represent higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
