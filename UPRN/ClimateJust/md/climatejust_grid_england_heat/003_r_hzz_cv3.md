### Average vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - High estimate (90th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, representing the result of an equally-weighted combination of community characteristics and projected physical heat exposure. It identifies geographic "hot spots" where high social vulnerability coincides with a high likelihood of exposure to extreme heat, indicating where negative impacts on health and wellbeing are most probable.

#### Description

The metric is composed of two primary elements, standardized as a z-score to allow for national comparison:

* **Average Vulnerability:** This component is the simple mean of **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** scores for all neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within a particular **25km grid cell** across England. It accounts for the personal, social, and environmental factors—such as age, health, and housing—that determine a community's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from heat events.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature (2050s):** This hazard-exposure metric utilizes projections for the average daily maximum temperatures during the summer months (June, July, and August) for the **2050s** time horizon.
* **High Estimate (90th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "high estimate" scenario where there is a **90% probability** that actual temperatures will be *below* this threshold (and a 10% probability they will be higher).
* **Standardization:** The final value is provided as a **standardized score (z-score)**. This indicates how far a grid cell deviates from the English average, with higher positive values representing higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
