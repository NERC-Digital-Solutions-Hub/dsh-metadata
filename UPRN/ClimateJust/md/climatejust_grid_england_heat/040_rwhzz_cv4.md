### Population weighted vulnerability and mean summer maximum temperature 2050s - Low estimate (10th percentile)

This field measures **heat disadvantage**, which identifies geographic "hot spots" where a community’s social vulnerability intersects with projected physical heat exposure. It accounts for both the likelihood of a neighborhood coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative impacts on community health and wellbeing.

#### Description

The metric represents an equally-weighted combination of socio-spatial vulnerability and projected temperature, provided as a **standardized score (z-score)** to allow for comparison across England:

* **Population Weighted Vulnerability:** This component represents **socio-spatial heat vulnerability** aggregated to a 25km grid cell. It is calculated by taking the mean scores of neighborhoods (MSOAs) located within the grid cell, using their population-weighted centre-points to allocate them to the correct cell. This accounts for factors like sensitivity (age and health), enhanced exposure, and the ability to prepare, respond, and recover.
* **Mean Summer Maximum Temperature 2050s:** This hazard-exposure metric focuses on the projected **average daily maximum temperature** during the summer months (June, July, and August). The "2050s" time horizon represents a 20-year average for the period from 2040–2060.
* **Low Estimate (10th Percentile):** Based on **UKCP09 probabilistic projections**, this represents a "low" or conservative estimate scenario. Statistically, it indicates there is a **90% probability** that the actual mean summer maximum temperature will be **higher** than this value.
* **Standardization:** The final value is a **z-score**, indicating how many standard deviations a grid cell deviates from the **English average**. Higher positive values indicate higher levels of climate disadvantage relative to the rest of the country.
