The field **'Relative heat Disadvantage, 1.5℃ warmer world'** (internal name `zarheatdisadvantagefuture15`) represents the final All-Relative Heat disadvantage score in a world with a 1.5°C global mean temperature increase.

### Description

This metric uses the All-Relative Z-score method to identify neighbourhoods where current social vulnerabilities will most severely intersect with projected 1.5°C heat hazards. It accounts for both the likelihood of coming into contact with high temperatures and the potential severity of negative health impacts.

* **Assumption:** Communities with high disadvantage scores are prioritized for adaptation as they face the greatest risk of well-being losses.
* **Vulnerability Dimension:** This is the primary output of the **Heat Exposure and Disadvantage** assessment for the 1.5°C scenario.
* **Caveat:** As an "All-Relative" score, it allows for a consistent comparison of disadvantage levels against the baseline and other future scenarios.
* **Data Source:** Calculated by combining projected 1.5°C UKCP18 extreme temperature data with the SSHVI.
