The field 'Summer extreme Relative Z-score maximum temperature low emissions 20 year return period 2080 90th percentile' (internal name z_ar_tasmax_rcp26_rp20_2080_90p) represents the standardized high estimate of projected late-century temperatures during a 1-in-20 year extreme heatwave under a low emissions scenario.
Description
This metric is an "All-Relative" Z-score, meaning it is calculated using the mean and standard deviation across all four considered temperature scenarios and all time periods (2020s, 2050s, 2080s) in the database.
 It converts the 90th percentile (high estimate) projection for late-century (2070–2090) daytime maximums during a 5% annual-probability extreme event into a standardized score.
 This identifies neighbourhoods facing acute late-century relative heat hazard exposure compared to the entire spectrum of climate futures in the database, even in a world with successful carbon mitigation.
Assumption: Higher relative maximum temperatures signify a greater potential for residents to experience catastrophic heat-related health and welfare impacts.
Vulnerability Dimension: Standardized input for the Hazard Exposure dimension.
Caveat: This is a high estimate; there is only a 10% probability that actual temperatures during such an event would exceed these values.
Data Source: Standardized from UKCP18 probabilistic projections.
